    How InboxKey handles your data

    This page describes what the extension does and does not do with your information. No claims to verify, just facts to read.

    What is accessed

    The only data the extension reads

    Verification emails only

    Only emails containing verification codes or magic links are read. All other emails are ignored entirely.

    Read-only access

    Email content is read but never modified, deleted, or moved. The extension cannot compose, send, or alter any email.

    Parsed locally

    All email parsing happens on your device. Email content is processed in memory and never leaves the browser.

    What is stored

    Everything saved locally, and nothing else

    Connection status

    Which email accounts are connected and whether the connection is active. No email content is stored.

    Preferences

    Display settings, blocked domains, and automation preferences. Stored in Chrome's local encrypted storage.

    Short-lived code cache

    Recently detected verification codes are held briefly so they can be offered for autofill. They expire automatically and can be cleared from Settings at any time.

    What is not done

    Things the extension will never do

    No analytics or tracking

    No usage analytics, no event tracking, no telemetry of any kind. There is nothing to opt out of because nothing is collected.

    No external connections

    The extension does not contact any server, API, or service other than your email provider. It does not phone home.

    No data leaves your device

    Nothing is uploaded, synced, or transmitted anywhere. All data stays in your browser's local storage on your machine.

    Permissions explained

    Every permission requested, and why

    identity
    Signs in with your Google account so verification emails can be read.
    tabs
    Detects login and verification pages so codes can be offered for autofill.
    activeTab
    Checks the current tab for code input fields so verification codes can be inserted.
    storage
    Saves preferences and settings locally in Chrome's encrypted storage.
    scripting
    Places the autofill chip on login pages so codes can be inserted into form fields.
    nativeMessaging
    Communicates with the local InboxBridge helper for IMAP accounts. Only active if installed.
